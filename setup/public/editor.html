How the editor works

Drag a part to move it, drag its handles to resize. Pink marks show what you have lined up with. Hold Ctrl or Shift while dragging to ignore snapping altogether. To move several parts at once, drag a box across them from an empty spot, or Ctrl+click each one. Then drag any of them. The settings they have in common are under Change all of them, and change every one at once. Click an empty spot on the canvas to select it, and drag its right or bottom edge to resize it. Ctrl+] bring forward  ·  Ctrl+[ send backward  ·  add Shift to go all the way ←↑↓→ nudge by 1px  ·  Shift + arrows by 10px  ·  Ctrl+Z undo  ·  Ctrl+Shift+Z redo
What the pink marks mean
  • A pink line - an edge or center is lined up with the canvas or another part.
  • A dashed outline with a label - you have matched that part's width or height exactly. The label says which part and what size.
  • Two pink blocks with a number - the gap you are making matches a gap you already have. Put the artist 10px under the title and the progress bar will catch at 10px under the artist.

None of it is compulsory: hold Ctrl or Shift while dragging and everything moves freely. The canvas shows "snapping off" while you do.

Hiding and locking parts

Every part has an eye in the list on the right, and a switch in its own settings. Hiding is not deleting - the part keeps all its settings and comes back exactly as it was. A hidden part is not on the canvas at all. Click it in the list to change it.

The padlock beside the eye locks a part in place. It cannot be dragged, resized or nudged, and clicks go straight through it to whatever is underneath. That makes a card behind everything easy to work over. Lock the canvas to keep its size.

The Canvas row has an eye too. Hiding it drops the background panel and its border, so the art and text sit straight over your gameplay. Your colors are kept for when you turn it back on.

Putting one part over another

Every part has a Layer row in its settings. Forward and Backward step it past the next part it overlaps, and To front and To back go all the way. Under the buttons it says what the part is over and under.

To make a part stick out past the edge of the panel, select the canvas and set Panel inset. The panel is drawn that far in from the edge, and anything in the space around it hangs over its edge.

Colors that follow the album art

The Vib, Dark, Light and Mute swatches are not fixed colors - they are taken from the artwork of whatever is playing, so the widget re-tints itself with every song.

Pick a color from the picker instead if you want it to stay put.

Sharing a theme, or using someone else's

A theme is one small file. Export writes it out, and you can send it to anyone running Queueify.

Import opens a file somebody sent you and drops it straight into the editor. It arrives as a new theme, so nothing you already have is overwritten - give it a name and save it.

Imported themes are checked the same way as everything else, so a file that has been messed with cannot put anything odd into your widget.

Saving, and putting it on stream

Save writes the theme to disk. Use on stream makes it the live one and reloads the widget in OBS for you.

Saving a theme that is already live updates the stream straight away. If OBS cannot be reached, the message says so in red and asks you to refresh the source yourself.
